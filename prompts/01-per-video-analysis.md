# Prompt 01 — Deep per-video analysis

Run once per video, with the full transcript pasted below the prompt.

---

You are an expert analyst of behavioral science, marketing psychology, and
Rory Sutherland's body of work (Vice Chairman of Ogilvy UK, author of
*Alchemy: The Surprising Power of Ideas That Don't Make Sense*). Below is the
full transcript of one video from the 37-part series "Rory Sutherland and the
Psychology of Irrational Value".

Read the ENTIRE transcript before answering. Do not skim. Then produce the
following analysis. Be specific and concrete — every claim you attribute to
Sutherland must be traceable to the transcript, and quotes must be verbatim
(include the nearest [mm:ss] timestamp).

## 1. Core thesis (2–3 sentences)
The single central argument of this video, stated as Sutherland would defend it.

## 2. The argument structure
Reconstruct his reasoning as a chain: premise → evidence → inference →
conclusion. Note where he argues by analogy or anecdote rather than data, and
whether the leap is justified.

## 3. Named psychological principles
Every behavioral-science concept invoked, explicitly or implicitly. For each:
- The formal name (e.g., costly signaling, hyperbolic discounting, goal
  dilution, focusing illusion, satisficing, loss aversion, social proof,
  efficiency vs. effectiveness trade-off, "psycho-logic")
- How Sutherland applies or twists it
- The originating researcher/school where identifiable (Kahneman, Thaler,
  Cialdini, Gigerenzer, Zahavi, Spence, etc.)

## 4. Case studies & examples
Every real-world example used (brands, products, policies, historical
episodes). For each: what happened, what it's evidence FOR, and whether the
example actually supports the claim or is doing rhetorical work.

## 5. Quotable lines
The 3–5 most distinctive verbatim quotes, with timestamps — the lines you'd
put on a slide.

## 6. Contrarian claims
Where Sutherland contradicts conventional business/economic wisdom. State the
orthodox view, his inversion, and the strongest steelman objection to him.

## 7. Practical applications
Concrete, actionable implications for (a) marketing/branding, (b) product and
pricing decisions, (c) everyday persuasion. Only applications actually
licensed by this video's argument — no generic advice.

## 8. Cross-reference hooks
Themes in this video likely to recur elsewhere in the series or in *Alchemy*
(e.g., "the opposite of a good idea can also be a good idea", signaling via
inefficiency, perception vs. reality engineering). One line each, so a later
synthesis pass can stitch videos together.

## 9. Critical evaluation (1 paragraph)
Where the argument is strongest, where it's weakest or unfalsifiable, and what
evidence would change the picture.
