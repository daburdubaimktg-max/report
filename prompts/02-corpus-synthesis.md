# Prompt 02 — Corpus synthesis across all 37 videos

Run once, after the per-video pass. Input: all 37 analyses from Prompt 01
(or all 37 full transcripts if your context window allows, ~130k tokens).

---

You have the complete analyses of all 37 videos in the series "Rory Sutherland
and the Psychology of Irrational Value". Synthesize them into a single
intellectual map of Sutherland's system of thought. This must be a true
synthesis — organized by IDEA, not by video — with video numbers cited as
evidence throughout (e.g., "V03, V17, V22").

## 1. The unified theory
In one page: what is Sutherland's overarching model of value? How do
perception, signaling, context, and "psycho-logic" fit together into one
framework? Where does he stand relative to orthodox economics and to
mainstream behavioral economics (he is NOT simply repeating Kahneman — locate
the difference precisely).

## 2. The recurring principles (ranked)
The 10–15 principles that recur across the series, ranked by how often and how
centrally they appear. For each: a crisp one-paragraph statement, the videos
where it appears, and his single best example of it.

## 3. The taxonomy of irrational value
Build the classification implicit in his examples: value created by signaling,
by scarcity, by story, by friction/effort, by context/framing, by uncertainty
reduction, etc. Place every major case study from the series into the taxonomy.

## 4. Contradictions and tensions
Where does Sutherland contradict himself across videos, or leave tensions
unresolved? Quote both sides.

## 5. The Sutherland method
Extract his repeatable creative procedure — the moves he makes to generate
"alchemy" (inversion, asking what a logical person would never propose,
changing perception instead of reality, adding rather than removing friction,
renaming the problem). State it as a step-by-step method someone could apply.

## 6. Playbook
The 15 most actionable, non-obvious tactics from the whole series for
marketing, pricing, product design, and communication — each with its
supporting video(s) and case study.

## 7. Study guide
A recommended viewing order in 4–5 thematic clusters (not the playlist order),
with one sentence per video on what it uniquely contributes.

## 8. Open questions
The 5 most interesting questions the series raises but does not answer.
